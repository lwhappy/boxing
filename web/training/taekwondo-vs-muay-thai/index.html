Taekwondo vs Muay Thai — Roundhouse Kick vs Knee Strike (Animated)

Taekwondo and Muay Thai are both kicking arts, but they win at completely different distances. The animated demonstration above shows the whole story with live health bars: the taekwondo fighter opens from long range and lands two full-contact roundhouse kicks that drop the Muay Thai fighter to 20% health — and then the Muay Thai fighter closes the distance with one explosive step and finishes the bout with a knee strike that takes the taekwondo fighter from 100% to zero.

Taekwondo rules at long range

A taekwondo roundhouse kick travels faster and further than any punch — the striking foot can exceed 1,000 N of peak force and arrives from more than a metre outside punching range. In the animated bout above, the taekwondo fighter scores twice with it: the first roundhouse snaps the Muay Thai fighter's health bar from 100% to 60%, and the second one — thrown before he can reset — cuts it again to 20%. That is the taekwondo game plan: stay long, chamber fast, and score with kicks before the opponent can ever reach you with a hand or a knee.

Muay Thai rules at knee range

Muay Thai is called "the art of eight limbs" for a reason — and the knee is its shortest, heaviest weapon. Once the distance closes, the geometry flips: a roundhouse kick needs room to arc, but a knee strike only needs a single step. In the animated demonstration, the Muay Thai fighter eats both kicks, then dashes forward with a long step straight into taekwondo's blind zone and drives a knee strike up through the middle. The taekwondo fighter's health bar drops from 100% to 0% on the spot and he collapses — a knockout with the shortest strike in the fight.

What the animated bout teaches

  • Taekwondo's plan: stay at kicking range, chamber and fire roundhouse kicks faster than the opponent can close, reset after every score.
  • Muay Thai's plan: walk through the range phase, then close with one committed step — inside kicking range the roundhouse dies and the knee owns the fight.
  • The deciding factor: distance control. The health bars in the animation show it plainly — taekwondo wins the scoring race from far away, Muay Thai wins the fight up close.
