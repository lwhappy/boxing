How to do a perfect push-up

A push-up is a plank that moves. Hands planted under the shoulders, the body locked in one straight line from heels to head, chest lowered to a fist's height, then the floor pressed away. The athlete on the left plays the full rep — the four callouts mark the checkpoints, and each muscle chip lights up the fibres that phase loads.

The four checkpoints

Every good rep passes the same four tests. The callouts on the stage point at each one while the athlete moves.

Hands

Palms flat under the shoulders, slightly wider than the chest, middle fingers forward. Press the whole hand in — every finger — so the wrists never carry the load alone.

Legs

Knees locked, feet hip-width, toes tucked under. The thighs stay squeezed for the entire set: loose legs are the reason hips sag first.

Hips

The pelvis rides the line — never dropped into an arched lower back, never piked toward the ceiling. Squeeze the glutes and the hips lock in place.

Waist

Head to heels is one straight line. Ribs pulled down, stomach braced as if expecting a tap — the waist must not bend at any point of the rep.

The rep, phase by phase

Slow is a skill: the athlete can play the whole rep at half speed from the stage controls.

1

Set up

Hands under the shoulders, legs locked, glutes and stomach tight. Checkpoints first, movement second — the line exists before the rep starts.

2

Lower

Bend the elbows to about 45° from the ribs — not straight out to the sides — and sink the chest toward the floor. In through the nose on the way down.

3

Bottom

Chest a fist's height from the floor, elbows tracking back at roughly 45°, body still one straight line. This is where sagging hips and flared elbows are born — hold the checkpoints.

4

Press

Push the floor away — the hands try to fold the ground in half — until the arms lock out. Out through the mouth on the press. The shoulders stay down; no shrugging at the top.

5

Reset & repeat

Re-tighten the line and start the next rep from the checkpoints. Quality reps first: five clean push-ups beat fifteen head-bobs every time.

Muscles the push-up works

Tap any of these on the stage to see exactly where each one sits on the athlete.

Chest prime mover

The pectorals pull the body off the floor and control the descent — most of the load lives here.

Triceps lockout

The back of the arm finishes every press; narrow hands shift even more work onto them.

Shoulders stabiliser

Front delts hold the shoulder blades down and back through the whole range.

Core anti-sag

Abs and obliques keep the ribs locked to the hips — the push-up trains the plank, not just the press.

Glutes hip lock

They pin the pelvis to the line so the hips neither sag nor pike.

Quads knees locked

They hold the knees straight and the heels pressing back through every rep.

Mistakes that waste a rep

Four errors do most of the damage — all of them visible on the stage the moment you look for them.

Flared elbows

Elbows out at a full 90° swing the load onto the shoulder capsules and strains them. Keep the upper arms at about 45° from the ribs — the arrow from the elbows should point back at your feet, not out at the walls.

Sagging hips

The moment the core lets go, the lower back arches and the lumbar spine takes load it was never asked to carry. If the hips drop, end the set — a shorter clean set builds more than a longer broken one.

Head bobbing

Reaching the nose for the floor fakes depth while the chest stays high. The chest — not the chin, not the nose — travels to a fist's height, and the neck stays long and in line.

Half reps

Bouncing a few centimetres down and back pumps out numbers but skips both halves of the work: the loaded descent and the triceps finish. Full range, slower, fewer — that is a push-up.
