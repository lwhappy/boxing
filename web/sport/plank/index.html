How to do a perfect plank

A plank is not a punishment — it is one straight line held against gravity. Elbows under the shoulders, heels pushed back, glutes squeezed, stomach braced: four anchors and nothing sags. The athlete on the left holds the full position — the four callouts mark the checkpoints, and each muscle chip lights up the fibres keeping him there.

The four checkpoints

Every good hold passes the same four tests. The callouts on the stage point at each one while the athlete holds.

Elbows

Directly under the shoulders, forearms flat ahead of them. The elbows are the load path — anywhere else and the shoulders or lower back pay for it.

Waist

Ribs pulled down, stomach braced in. The midsection is the bridge between shoulders and hips — it must never sag toward the floor.

Hips

Level with shoulders and heels the whole hold. Sagging strains the lower back; spiking up steals the work away from the core.

Heels

Pushed actively back with the knees locked. The legs are not passengers — pressing the heels back is what locks the line in place.

The hold, phase by phase

Slow is a skill: the athlete can play the hold at half speed from the stage controls.

1

Set up

Kneel, forearms down, elbows under the shoulders. Step the feet back one at a time until the legs are long.

2

Build the line

Push the heels back and squeeze the thighs until the body reads as one straight board — head to heels, no kinks.

3

Brace

Ribs down, stomach pulled in, glutes squeezed. This trio is the plank: everything else is just furniture.

4

Hold & breathe

Keep steady nose breaths going — never hold the air. The moment the hips sag or the shoulders shrug, the quality seconds are over.

5

Exit clean

Drop the knees to the floor before form breaks. Rest, re-check the four checkpoints, then go again. Twenty clean seconds beat two sagging minutes.

Muscles the plank works

Tap the muscle chips above — or the labels on the idle stage — to light each one up on the athlete.

Core prime mover

Abs and obliques brace the ribs to the pelvis — the entire exercise lives or dies here.

Glutes hip lock

Squeezed glutes pin the pelvis level so the hips can neither sag nor spike during the hold.

Quads leg lock

Locked quads straighten the knees and keep pushing the heels back — the line stays taut.

Shoulders girdle

The delts hold the shoulder blades down and propped so the chest never collapses between them.

Triceps arm lock

Firm triceps keep the elbows quiet and stop the shoulders shrugging up around the ears.

Chest upper prop

The pecs keep the chest lifted off the floor while the forearms quietly take the weight.

Mistakes that turn planks into back pain

Four errors do most of the damage — all of them visible on the stage the moment you look for them.

Sagging hips

The most common plank on earth. Once the hips drop below the line the lower back takes the load and the core checks out. Cue it as "pull the belly button toward the spine" and restart the hold the moment it happens.

Piking up

Hips spiked toward the ceiling look tidy but remove the core from the equation — the legs and arms hold a tent instead. If a straight line feels impossible, hold fewer seconds and build up honestly.

Head dropping

Letting the head hang breaks the line at the neck and rounds the upper back. Keep the neck long, eyes at the floor about a hand's length ahead — the head is just the end of the board.

Holding too long

Grinding past form failure trains sagging, not strength. When the hips drift or the breath gets choppy, the set is done. Multiple short, clean holds beat one long, collapsing one.

A braced trunk is what lets a fighter absorb a body shot and keep throwing — the hold you are training here is the same brace a boxer carries for three minute rounds. See it tested at real range in the boxing vs kickboxing bout.
