Technique & tradeoffs

Find the rhythm. Understand the cost.

The animated version uses a jump-back / jump-in transition. “Jump up” means bringing the feet back in, not adding a separate vertical jump.

Animated in this version

Jump-back / jump-in

Both feet travel together into the floor position and back underneath the body before the broad jump.

Why use it

  • Rhythm: a repeatable down–up–forward sequence.
  • Symmetry: both legs share the same transition.
  • Clear boundary: the palm line makes the jump-in position easy to inspect.
Tradeoffs

More bounce. More demand.

A quick transition is not automatically the most sustainable one.

  • Higher impact: repeated jump-backs, jump-ins and landings need control.
  • Shoulder demand: the arms and shoulders repeatedly support and redirect bodyweight.
  • Fatigue: shorten the jump rather than chase distance with stiff or unstable landings.
Alternative technique

Step-back / step-in

Comparison only, not animated in this version.

Move one foot at a time into and out of the floor position, then set up the broad jump.

  • Potential benefit: a less abrupt transition and a more deliberate pace.
  • Tradeoff: more foot contacts and an alternating-leg pattern; the broad jump still has impact.

Study tool, not a judging standard

The 20 m shuttle is a demonstration layout, not a claim about every venue. The 30 cm annotation illustrates this animation only. This is an instructional visualization, not official HYROX judging guidance; use the applicable event rules and briefing for competition requirements.
