How to do a perfect air squat

An air squat is the moving handshake between you and the floor. Feet planted, hips pushed back and down until the crease passes the knees, chest proud the whole way, then the floor pushed away to stand tall. The athlete on the left plays the full rep — the four callouts mark the checkpoints, and each muscle chip lights up the fibres that phase loads.

The four checkpoints

Every good rep passes the same four tests. The callouts on the stage point at each one while the athlete moves.

Chest

Proud chest, ribs pulled down, spine one long line from tail to skull. The torso can lean forward — it must never fold.

Hips

Push the hips back and down, not straight down. Depth is the hip crease passing the knees — anything higher is a half rep.

Knees

Track over the toes, pushed outward, never caving in. The heels stay glued to the floor through the entire rep.

Feet

Shoulder-width with the toes turned out about 15°. The whole foot stays planted — weight balanced mid-foot, not on the toes.

The rep, phase by phase

Slow is a skill: the athlete can play the whole rep at half speed from the stage controls.

1

Stand tall

Feet set, weight mid-foot, stomach braced. Checkpoints first, movement second — the squat starts before the hips move.

2

Brace & sit back

Push the hips back and let the knees bend over the toes. The arms swing straight out front as counterweight — in through the nose on the way down.

3

Bottom

Hip crease below the knees, chest proud, heels flat, lower back neutral. This is where the glutes and quads load hardest — and where caving knees and rounding backs are born.

4

Drive up

Push the floor away through the whole foot — mid-foot and heels together — until the hips and knees straighten. Out through the mouth; squeeze the glutes at the top.

5

Reset & repeat

Re-check the four points and start the next rep. Quality first: five deep, clean squats beat twenty knee-bobs every time.

Muscles the air squat works

Tap the muscle chips above — or the labels on the idle stage — to light each one up on the athlete.

Quads prime mover

The thighs control the descent and straighten the knees on the drive — most of the load lives here.

Glutes hip drive

Loaded hardest at the bottom, the glutes extend the hips and stand you back up to full height.

Core anti-fold

A braced stomach locks the ribs to the pelvis so the lower back never rounds under you at depth.

Chest proud torso

Leading with the chest keeps the torso upright instead of folding forward over the knees.

Triceps counterbalance

The straightened arms act as a counterweight, helping the weight stay balanced over mid-foot.

Shoulders arm carriage

Relaxed shoulders stay down and back so the arms stay long — no shrugging as you sink.

Mistakes that waste a rep

Four errors do most of the damage — all of them visible on the stage the moment you look for them.

Knees caving in

When the knees drift inward the inner thigh takes over and the knee joint pays for it. Cue the squat as "knees out over the pinky toes" and push the floor apart on the way up.

Heels lifting

Weight rolling to the toes tips you forward and steals the glutes out of the drive. If the heels float, widen the stance a touch, turn the toes out more, and sit back into the hips instead of down onto the knees.

Rounded lower back

Losing the brace at depth rounds the lumbar spine and loads the discs instead of the hips. Breathe in, brace like you are about to be tapped on the stomach, and keep the chest leading the whole way down.

Half-depth reps

Bobbing above parallel skips the loaded bottom half where the glutes do their work. Depth is the hip crease passing the knees — full range, slower, fewer: that is a squat.
