How to do a farmer's carry

Pick up something heavy, walk, and refuse to be folded in half. That is the whole farmer's carry — and it is the single best test of grip, trunk and posture in one movement. The four checkpoints

Everything that goes wrong in a carry shows up at one of these four points. The callouts track them while the athlete walks.

Start

Deadlift the dumbbells to full hip and knee extension. Stand between them, hinge down and set the handle in the middle of each palm, then drive through the whole foot — a half stand is where the round-back reps happen.

Spine

Maintain a neutral spine and contract the abdominals. Ribs stacked over the pelvis, stomach braced as if you expect a tap — that brace is what stops the weights folding you sideways or tipping you into a lean.

Chest

Keep the chest up and the shoulders back and packed. The load wants to roll them forward and drag them out of the socket — a long neck and quiet arms are the tell that they stayed put.

Feet

Take quick, smooth steps with the whole foot planted. No bounce, no crossing over, no leaning away from the heavier side — the trunk stays a column while the legs march.

The carry, step by step

The full walk plays on the stage — drop it to half speed to watch the feet and the grip.

1

Set the grip

Stand between the weights, hinge at the hips, and set the handle in the middle of each palm. Hook the fingers, stack the wrist. This is the detail that decides whether the last ten metres happen.

2

Stand tall

Drive through the whole foot and stand up with the load at your sides. Pack the shoulders down, brace the stomach, and take one breath before the first step.

3

Walk

Short steps, quiet landings, whole foot down. The trunk does not sway and the arms do not swing — the legs march underneath a braced column.

4

Turn

Turn with your feet rather than twisting the spine. The load keeps moving in the direction you are facing, and the brace stays on through the change of direction.

5

Set it down

Hinge and place the weights under control. Dropping them from standing is how a good carry becomes a tweaked back.

Muscles the farmer's carry works

Tap the muscle chips above — or the labels on the small stage — to light each one up on the athlete.

Forearms the limiter

Grip endurance is what ends a carry, not the legs. Fingers, wrist flexors and the forearm flexors hold the load shut rep after rep.

Traps the yoke

Upper traps and rhomboids keep the shoulders pinned down and back while two heavy weights try to drag them out of the socket.

Core the brace

Abs and obliques lock the ribcage over the pelvis so the load cannot fold you sideways or pull you into a lean.

Shoulders stabilisers

The delts keep the arms packed and quiet, resisting the swing that wants to pull the weights away from the body.

Glutes pelvic level

The glutes hold the pelvis level under load and drive each short, controlled step of the walk.

Quads the march

Quads take the load down and up on every step — quiet feet and no bounce keep the work in the muscle instead of the joints.

Mistakes that end a carry early

Four errors cost most people the last twenty metres — and all of them are visible the moment you look for them.

Letting the shoulders shrug

As the grip tires, the traps take over and the shoulders creep up to the ears. That shortens the neck, pinches the upper back and wastes the very muscles you need later. Cue it as "pockets heavy" — shoulders down, arms long.

Leaning away from the load

If one side is heavier, or one grip gives out first, the trunk tilts and the spine takes a shear it was never built for. Keep the weights matched, and if you must run them uneven, alternate sides every set.

Long, loud steps

Reaching for distance turns the carry into a lunge and bounces the load. Short steps, quiet landings, whole foot down — the point is time under load, not speed across the gym.

Chasing weight over time

The carry is a grip-endurance test: 40 seconds with a modest load beats eight seconds with a heavy one. Build to 40–60 seconds of clean walking before you add plates.
