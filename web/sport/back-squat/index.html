How to do a perfect back squat

The barbell back squat is the king of the weight room — and the most mistimed lift in it. Bar resting on the rear-delts shelf, hips pushed back and down below the knees, chest proud, the bar travelling a straight vertical line over mid-foot. The athlete on the left plays the full rep under the bar — the four callouts mark the checkpoints, and each muscle chip lights up the fibres that phase loads.

The four checkpoints

Every good rep passes the same four tests. The callouts on the stage point at each one while the athlete moves.

Bar

Rests on the rear-delts shelf just below the neck — never on the spine or the neck itself. The hands pull the bar down into the groove; the elbows stay under the bar, not flared behind it.

Hips

Push the hips back and down, not straight down. Depth is the hip crease passing the knees — and the bar must stay stacked over mid-foot the whole way.

Knees

Track over the toes and stay pushed outward — under load they will find every excuse to cave in. The knees also never drift past where the hips put them.

Feet

Shoulder-width with toes turned out slightly. Three points of contact — big toe, little toe, heel — glued to the floor. The bar rides over mid-foot like it is on a rail.

The rep, phase by phase

Slow is a skill: the athlete can play the whole rep at half speed from the stage controls.

1

Set up

Duck under the bar, set it on the rear-delts shelf, pull it down into the shoulders with the hands. Everything tight before a single step back.

2

Unrack & brace

Big breath into the stomach, brace like you are bracing for a punch, then walk the bar back. Two steps, set the stance, squeeze the bar one more time.

3

Sit back & down

Hips and chest break together — the hips travel back while the knees travel forward over the toes. The bar stays glued over mid-foot: that vertical line is the rep.

4

Drive up

At depth, push the floor away through mid-foot and heels. Hips and shoulders rise at the same speed — if the hips shoot up first, the bar ends up in front of the knees and the good-morning begins.

5

Finish & rerack

Stand all the way up, breathe at the top, reset the brace. Rerack by stepping in and dropping the bar onto the hooks — never by bending forward under load.

Muscles the back squat works

Tap the muscle chips above — or the labels on the idle stage — to light each one up on the athlete.

Quads prime mover

They bend the knees on the way down and straighten them under the whole load on the drive — the biggest share of the work lives here.

Glutes hip drive

Loaded hardest in the hole, the glutes extend the hips and finish the lockout. Deep reps recruit them far more than half reps ever will.

Core the brace

A braced stomach pressurises the torso so the bar's load travels down the spine to the hips — not through a rounding lower back.

Chest upright torso

A proud chest keeps the upper back tight and the bar path vertical instead of folding you forward onto your toes.

Shoulders bar shelf

The rear delts and traps form the shelf the bar rests on — a tight shelf means a quiet bar and a straight bar path.

Triceps elbow lock

Firm triceps pin the bar into the shoulder groove and keep the elbows from drifting — the bar stays where you set it.

Mistakes that turn heavy into hurt

Four errors do most of the damage — all of them visible on the stage the moment you look for them.

Bar on the neck

Resting the bar on the spine or the top of the traps concentrates the whole load on the neck vertebrae. Set it on the rear-delts shelf below the neck and pull it down with the hands — the shelf, not the bone, carries the weight.

Hips shooting up

When the hips rise faster than the shoulders the lift turns into a good-morning: the bar drifts in front of the knees and the lower back takes over. Cue "chest and hips rise together" and keep the bar stacked over mid-foot.

Knees caving in

Under load the knees hunt for the easiest path — inward. The cue is "knees out over the pinky toes" on both the descent and the drive, and pushing the floor apart as you stand.

Losing the brace

Exhaling everything at the bottom deflates the torso: the spine rounds and the discs pay for it. Breathe behind the brace — big air in, short exhales at the top between reps, never at depth.
