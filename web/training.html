How it works

The black fighter carries two target points — one at the head, one at the chest. Every punch is assigned to a target: jab and cross drive into the body mark, while both hooks and both uppercuts aim for the head. The engine finds the moment in each clip where the striking glove reaches that mark, steps the red boxer into range, and lands the glove on it — plus a flash and an impact sound on contact. When a punch tops out below head height, the black boxer ducks into it instead of the red one floating off the floor.

Combo tips

Classic starters: jab → cross (the one-two), jab → lead hook, and jab → rear uppercut. Jabs and lead-hand shots travel shortest, so they measure the distance; rear-hand shots carry the power. Two same-hand punches in a row are slower — alternate lead and rear hands to keep the chain flowing. Punch selection order is the order they are thrown, so build from light to heavy.
