Jab-Rear Uppercut-Lead Hook

Power uppercut from the rear hand, finished with a lead hook to the jaw.

Combo 1-6-3 Three punches Power uppercut Hook finish Sequence: idle → jab → rear uppercut → lead hook

The sequence

The jab sets distance and disrupts the guard. Instead of following with a cross, the rear hand drops and fires an uppercut — driving up from the floor through the rear leg, hip, and fist. The uppercut's recoil loads the lead side, and the lead hook snaps around as the opponent's head snaps back from the uppercut.

When to use

When the opponent leans forward or ducks after the jab — the rear uppercut catches them coming in, lifting their chin into the perfect position for the lead hook. This combo is devastating against pressure fighters who slip forward to avoid the cross.

Key detail

The rear uppercut is the heaviest uppercut you own — the rear leg, hip, and shoulder all drive up in one line. The transition to the lead hook is natural: the uppercut's upward force rocks the opponent's head back, and the hook comes around the side while their weight is on their heels.

Common mistake

Throwing the uppercut from too far away. The rear uppercut is a short-range punch — if you throw it at cross range, it becomes an arm punch with no power. Step in with the jab to close distance, then fire the uppercut from inside.
