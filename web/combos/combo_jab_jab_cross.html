Jab-Jab-Cross

Double jab to disrupt rhythm, cross to capitalize on the opening.

Combo 1-1-2 Three punches Double jab Rhythm breaker Sequence: idle → jab → jab → cross

The sequence

First jab measures distance and occupies the guard. Second jab — thrown faster, not harder — disrupts the opponent's timing and pulls their attention fully to the lead side. The cross arrives a beat later, shooting through the center as the opponent's eyes are still tracking the second jab.

When to use

Against an opponent who reads single jabs and counters. The second jab breaks their counter rhythm — they expected one jab, committed to a response, and now the cross is already on its way. Also excellent against a taller fighter who leans back to avoid the jab.

Key detail

The two jabs are not identical. The first is a measuring jab — relaxed, informational. The second is a disruptor — snappier, with a half-step forward. The cross then commits fully, riding the forward momentum from the second jab's step.

Common mistake

Throwing both jabs at the same speed and then pausing before the cross. The combo works because of rhythm disruption — if all three punches are thrown at the same tempo, the opponent can simply shell up and wait. Vary the cadence: slow-fast-power.
