Jab-Cross-Lead Hook

The full 1-2-3 from idle guard: the bread-and-butter three-punch combination.

Combo 1-2-3 Three punches Classic combo All ranges Sequence: idle → jab → cross → lead hook

The sequence

From the idle guard, the jab snaps out first — lead hand, straight line, back to chin. The cross follows immediately, driving through the gap the jab opened. As the cross returns, the lead hook fires in a tight arc around the guard. Three punches, three angles, one continuous motion.

When to use

This is the most versatile combination in boxing. Use it at long range (the jab measures, the cross and hook follow if in range), at mid range (all three land clean), or as an entry combination to close distance. It works against any guard because it attacks from straight, straight, and circular angles.

Key detail

Each punch loads the next. The jab's recoil sets the cross's hip rotation. The cross's hip turn loads the lead side for the hook. The hook's arc naturally brings the guard back to center. The whole combo flows like a single motion — no resets, no pauses, no telegraphing between punches.

Common mistake

Throwing the hook as a separate action after the cross. The hook should begin as the cross is returning — both fists are moving at the same time. If you finish the cross, pause, then start the hook, you've given the opponent a full beat to recover and counter.
