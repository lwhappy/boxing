Jab-Cross

The classic 1-2, doubled: lead-hand jab to occupy the guard, rear-hand cross to land the power — then the same pair again before the opponent can reset.

Combo 1-2-1-2 Four punches Entry combo Lead + rear Sequence: idle → jab → cross → jab → cross

The sequence

Start from idle guard. The jab snaps out first — lead hand, straight line, back to chin. The instant the jab returns, the rear hip pivots and the cross fires through the gap the jab just opened. Then, without pausing to reset, the same 1-2 comes again: the second jab re-occupies the guard while the opponent is still tracking the first cross, and the second cross lands behind it. Four punches, one continuous motion.

When to use

This is the first combination every boxer learns and the most thrown combo in the sport — and doubling it is the classic trap. The first 1-2 asks the question: it draws the guard tight or forces a flinch. The second pair exploits the answer: the jab blinds, the cross lands while the guard is still recovering. Use it to establish distance, test the guard, and score.

Key detail

The transitions are seamless — no pause, no reset. The jab hand comes back as the cross hand goes out, both fists moving at the same time in opposite directions; and the second jab starts while the first cross is still returning, so the rhythm never drops. The hips rotate through all four punches in one continuous motion.

Common mistake

Pausing between the two pairs. The moment you reset and reload, the opponent gets a free breath to slip or counter. Keep the rhythm continuous: one-two, one-two — not one… two… one… two.
